Settings & data

🔒 Your fishing stays on this phone

No account, and no server keeps your data. Catches, photos, notes and spots are stored only here and leave only when you share them. Weather, maps and stocking are looked up by a water's location or the map area you view, never with your catches. See exactly what leaves your device.

Your data

Everything is stored only in this browser on this device. There is no account and no server. Back up regularly, and use a backup file to move your data to another device.

What leaves your device

  • Open-Meteo (weather/wind): the lake's coordinates. Today, Plan a trip and the Wind on my lakes map layer send each of your starred or saved waters' coordinates, one request per water. The Game plan (its tab and the line on Today) also asks Open-Meteo for the last 30 days of air temperature at the water's coordinates to estimate the water temperature, once a day per water.
  • USGS Water Services (flow, level, water temp): a box around the lake.
  • Map tiles (OpenStreetMap / Esri / USGS The National Map, basemap.nationalmap.gov): the map area you view, or, when you save a water for offline or tap Ready for no signal, the lake's area plus a strip along its walk in and any GPX routes you imported.
  • Windy / MyRadar: only when you open those panels or links.
  • National Weather Service (weather alerts): the lake's coordinates, and on Today each starred water's.
  • USGS statistics (flow vs normal): the gauge's id. The river rising line asks for that gauge's last three hours the same way.
  • Open-Meteo air quality (air-quality-api.open-meteo.com, smoke on the Your day card and 📴 Ready): the water's coordinates, when that card is shown or you get ready; kept on your device for no signal. Drive and walk times for the day plan are typed by you and stay on your phone; no map or routing service sees where you live.
  • RainViewer (rain radar): the map area you view, only while the radar layer is on.
  • California stocking and lake levels come from this app's own site (refreshed nightly from CDFW and CDEC), so nothing extra is sent.
  • Nevada stocking, fishing reports and lake levels come from this app's own site too (refreshed nightly from NDOW, USGS and Reclamation).
  • Oregon stocking, Recreation Report, lake levels, algae advisories and regulations come from this app's own site too (refreshed from ODFW, OHA, USGS, the Army Corps and Reclamation).
  • Regulations, toxic algae and fish advisories, ramps and facilities also come from this app's own site (refreshed from Title 14 CCR, the Water Boards, OEHHA and OpenStreetMap).
  • Getting there (roads, trails, land manager, description): the coordinates of the water (or of the spot you pick on its shore) go to the U.S. Forest Service, the Bureau of Land Management, USGS PAD-US (ArcGIS Online), OpenStreetMap's Overpass API, Wikipedia and Open-Meteo (elevations along the walk in), only when you open that tab; the answers are kept on your device for 90 days. Its Today card sends the same coordinates to NRCS SNOTEL and Open-Meteo (snow), the national interagency fire feed (NIFC, on ArcGIS Online) and InciWeb, kept for 3 hours. Your access notes stay on your device unless you share them with your crew.
  • Camping (campgrounds, dispersed camping, land manager): the water's coordinates go to the Forest Service, BLM, Recreation.gov, California State Parks, OpenStreetMap and PAD-US when you open that tab. Camp trips check open sites by sending Recreation.gov only each campground's id and the months of your nights; where you camp never leaves the phone, and drive times are typed by you or estimated on the phone.
  • Roads in (Getting there, and Ready for no signal): the app downloads Caltrans's whole chain-control and closure lists for California's mountain districts (cwwp2.dot.ca.gov) and picks out the ones near the water on the phone, so nothing about the water or you is sent. Nevada's and Oregon's road maps (nvroads.com, tripcheck.com) open only when you tap them.
  • Game plan (fish seen nearby): the water's coordinates go to iNaturalist (api.inaturalist.org), and the species photos load from iNaturalist's servers, kept on the phone for 7 days. Trailheads & parking (Getting there, and the map layer while it's on): the water's coordinates, or the map area you view, go to OpenStreetMap's Overpass API.
  • Last gas, ice and bait: the water's coordinates go to OpenStreetMap's Overpass API (overpass-api.de), kept on the phone for 30 days.
  • Ramp levels compare the lake level this app already has with ramp levels built into the app or typed by you: nothing extra is sent. Regs booklets open the state's PDF (wildlife.ca.gov, eregulations.com) only when you tap them.
  • Hands-free voice logging (off until you turn it on): your phone's speech recognition turns what you say into words, and on most phones that sound goes to Google (Android) or Apple (iPhone). Nothing else goes with it.
  • Plant calendars: if you subscribe, your calendar app fetches that water's public plant feed from this app's site.
  • Ocean spots (piers, jetties, beaches, marine protected areas and tide stations) come from this app's own site (refreshed weekly from OpenStreetMap, CDFW, ODFW and NOAA). On a pier or beach's page, NOAA Tides & Currents gets only the nearest tide station's id; Open-Meteo Marine (waves, swell, sea temperature) and the National Weather Service (marine zone, Coastal Waters Forecast, advisories) get the spot's coordinates. The answers are kept on your device so tides and the marine forecast work with no signal.
  • Directions links open Google Maps with that spot's location, only when you tap one.
  • Crew trips: a group plan (camp, meet-up, people, gear, license ticks) leaves the phone only when you send it to a friend, as a file or QR code. Where should we camp? ranks camps on the phone from distances you type; nothing is sent.

Catches, notes, photos, spots and depth data never leave the device. Sharing with your crew sends only what you pick, directly as a file or inside a QR code's link (the part after #, which browsers never send to a website).
